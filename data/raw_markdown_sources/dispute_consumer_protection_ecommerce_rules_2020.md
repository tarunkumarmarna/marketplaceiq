# Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020

## Legal Basis
Notified by the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution on July 23, 2020, under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. These rules apply to every e-commerce entity selling to Indian consumers — marketplace model (Flipkart, Amazon, Meesho) and inventory model alike — regardless of where the entity is registered.

## Grievance Redressal Mechanism
Every e-commerce entity must:
- Appoint a Nodal Officer or senior designated functionary resident in India, responsible for coordinating compliance.
- Appoint a Grievance Officer, whose name, contact details, and designation must be displayed on the platform.
- Acknowledge any consumer complaint within 48 hours of receipt.
- Resolve the complaint within 1 month of receipt.
- Provide a ticket number for every complaint so the consumer can track status.

Sellers on the platform (not just the platform operator) are separately required to appoint their own grievance officer with the same 48-hour/1-month timelines.

## Restrictions on E-Commerce Entities
- Cannot impose cancellation charges on consumers unless the entity itself bears an equivalent charge when it cancels an order.
- Must obtain explicit, affirmative consumer consent for a purchase — pre-ticked checkboxes or other default-opt-in "dark patterns" are prohibited.
- Cannot manipulate pricing to gain unreasonable profit.
- Cannot discriminate between consumers of the same class.
- Must not adopt unfair trade practices, in the course of business or otherwise.

## Mandatory Disclosures
Platforms must clearly display: legal name, office address(es), website details, and grievance officer contact information. For imported goods, the importer/seller's name and details must also be shown. Sellers must display total price (with a breakup of charges), expiry date, country of origin, and warranty/guarantee terms.

## Liability for Defective Goods (Inventory Model)
If an inventory-based e-commerce entity (one that owns the goods it sells, as opposed to a pure marketplace connecting third-party sellers) sells a defective product, it is treated as the seller/manufacturer and bears full liability — this liability is heavier than the equivalent marketplace-model scenario, where liability chiefly rests with the third-party seller.
