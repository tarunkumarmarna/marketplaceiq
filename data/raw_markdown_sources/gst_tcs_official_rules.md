# GST TCS Rules for E-Commerce Operators (Section 52, CGST Act)

## What TCS Is
Every registered e-commerce operator in India — including Flipkart, Amazon India, and Meesho — is legally required to collect Tax Collected at Source (TCS) from sellers on its platform. This obligation comes from Section 52 of the CGST Act, 2017, and applies uniformly to every marketplace operator, not just to one company's internal policy.

## Rate and Calculation
The current TCS rate is 0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST for intra-state sales, or 1% IGST for inter-state sales, applied to the *net* value of taxable supplies (i.e., after deducting returns and cancellations, and excluding GST itself from the base). This rate was reduced from an earlier 1% total rate via CGST Notification 15/2024, effective July 10, 2024.

Example: if a seller sells a product for ₹1,000 plus ₹180 GST, TCS is calculated on the ₹1,000 taxable value, not the ₹1,180 total.

## Who Must Register
Under Section 24 of the CGST Act, any seller supplying goods through an e-commerce operator must register for GST — there is no turnover-based exemption for this category, unlike standalone small businesses which enjoy a ₹40 lakh threshold exemption. This applies identically whether the seller lists on Flipkart, Amazon, or Meesho.

## Filing Process
1. The e-commerce operator deducts TCS at the time of paying the seller.
2. The operator deposits the TCS with the government by the 10th of the following month.
3. The operator files Form GSTR-8, reporting the TCS collected.
4. The credit reflects in the seller's electronic cash ledger and appears in their GSTR-2A, which the seller can use to offset their own output GST liability via GSTR-3B.

## Applicability Across Platforms
This is not company-specific policy — the same Section 52 obligation applies to Flipkart, Amazon India, Meesho, and every other registered e-commerce operator in India. Differences between platforms are mostly operational (e.g., where in each seller dashboard the TCS certificate appears), not legal.
